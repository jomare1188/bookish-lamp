## 12. Conserved-subnetwork GO enrichment — H0 sanity check (`top_GO_conserved.r`)

Do the genes on cross-species-conserved edges (step 7) carry coherent
biology, or is the conservation call noise? Enrichment (topGO, classic Fisher +
BH) of each species' conserved genes **against its own network nodes as
background** (not the genome — this removes the generic "co-expressed genes
differ from the genome" effect), then the enriched-term lists are compared
across species. Interest sets are fully contained in each background
(sugarcane 3,671/8,175 GO-annotated nodes; purple 3,963/12,220).

| Ontology | Sugarcane terms | Purple terms | Shared | Unique sug. | Unique pur. | Jaccard |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BP | 287 | 392 | 172 | 115 | 220 | 0.339 |
| CC | 82  | 106 | 59  | 23  | 47  | 0.457 |
| MF | 119 | 154 | 62  | 57  | 92  | 0.294 |

**Shared = conserved core machinery**, coherent and independently significant
in both: translation (cytosolic ribosome), regulated proteolysis (proteasome),
pre-mRNA splicing, photosynthesis/plastid and the C4 carbon core — **PEPCase,
PEP carboxykinase, malate dehydrogenase** — hormone/stimulus perception +
MAPK, and **glutamate synthase (GOGAT)**. So the primary-metabolism layer of
the N response (PEPCase, GOGAT — the exact players both source papers
spotlight) is the part that conserves. CC is the most conserved layer (physical
complexes > processes > activities).

**Species-specific conserved wiring** diverges into stress/defense: sugarcane
→ RNA-helicase/RNA-processing, hypersensitive response, electrical signaling
(action potential); purple → cytosolic translation, vesicle transport, cell
wall biogenesis, **iron-ion-starvation/siderophore**, and **malate synthase
(glyoxylate cycle)** — the iron-starvation and glyoxylate themes each map back
to a phenomenon its source paper described.

**Circadian rhythm** (GO:0007623) is annotated (27 sugarcane / 28 purple genes)
and present in the conserved sets, but is **not enriched** — it sits at
background rate (sug 0.19% vs 0.26%; pur 0.33% vs 0.22%). Enrichment measures
concentration, not conservation: a uniformly-present, uniformly-conserved
module reads as "not enriched" (also only 7–13 genes, little FDR power).

**Verdict:** the OrthoFinder R570↔LA-purple bridge captures real, shared
biology — the near-zero *edge/gene-level* conservation (steps 6–10) is a
genuine finding, not a mapping artifact. This test is conditioned on
topological conservation, so it does **not** by itself establish
"function conserved > topology conserved"; it shows the conserved topology
concentrates on the deeply-conserved primary-metabolism/core-machinery backbone.

Output: `files/network_conservation/enrichment_conserved/` —
per-species tables/plots under `sugarcane/` and `purple/`, plus
`GO_{BP,CC,MF}_conserved_{shared_terms,unique_sugarcane,unique_purple,comparison_summary}.csv`.

**Semantic convergence (`GO_semanthinc_enrichment.r`).** Rather than merging
the species, each species' full enriched set is placed in **one common
GO-semantic space** (GOSemSim Wang) built from their union (BP: 507 terms, 172
shared), clustered into 12 macro-themes. Plotted **faceted by species**, the two
panels are structurally near-identical — sugarcane and purple **independently**
enrich the *same* basic-process themes (membrane organization, intracellular
transport, plant-type cell-wall biogenesis, RNA-Pol-II transcription, malate /
ferredoxin / glucan metabolism, RNA splicing, detection of stimulus, response to
organonitrogen compound), and every theme carries a "both-species" fraction.
This is the figure backing the "conserved topology = basic-process backbone"
reading: functional enrichment converges even though the underlying edges do
not (the 12-macro-theme grouping is a visualisation choice, not a discovered
optimum). Output under `enrichment_conserved/semantic/`:
`GO_<ont>_conserved_semantic_space_by_species.{png,pdf}` (faceted convergence),
`GO_<ont>_conserved_semantic_convergence.{png,pdf}` (per-theme
sugarcane/both/purple bar), `..._clusters.csv`, `GO_conserved_semantic_summary.csv`.

## 14. Locating ScMYB61.1 in our data (`scripts/myb61/`) — H1 step 2

### 14a. The published id does not resolve

Kiet et al. give **no sequences**, and their gene id `Soff.09G0002230–3D`
cannot be mapped onto the LA purple annotation — the same reference they state
they used:

- In all **241,263** LA purple gene ids the haplotype suffix number equals the
  chromosome number (`…09G0003220-**9**G`, `…03D0011800-**3**D`) — 241,263
  matches, **0 exceptions**. A chr9 locus carrying a `-3D` suffix is therefore
  structurally impossible: the id is chimeric or mistyped.
- **Neither single-field repair lands on a MYB.** Keeping chr9,
  `Soffic.09G0002230-9{A,B,C,D,G}` show *no Myb signal even at a permissive
  `E<10`* (`--cut_ga` is far stricter); their only Pfam hits are
  `Transposase_21`, `Transpos_assoc` (‑9B) and `DUF1218` (‑9G) — TE-contaminated
  gene models. Keeping the suffix, `Soffic.03G0002230-3D` is a 230 aa protein
  with no Myb signal and no Arabidopsis hit at all.
- The orthogroup bridge agrees: copies of locus `09G0002230` **never** share an
  orthogroup with any confirmed MYB61 copy.

The two studies also classify TFs by different methods — Kiet used **PlantTFDB**
(homology to a curated database), this project uses **Pfam GA + PlnTFDB rules**
(§13) — so the label alone was never transferable. MYB61 was therefore
re-derived from sequence.

### 14b. MYB61 found in both references, by homology

Anchor **AtMYB61 = AT1G09540** → reciprocal best hit into sorghum and rice (each
returns the expected **pair** of grass whole-genome-duplication co-orthologues;
all 5 anchors verified as 2-repeat R2R3-MYBs by our own HMM library) → DIAMOND
blastp into both proteomes → two independent filters: reciprocal best hit back
to Arabidopsis must return AT1G09540 (this is what separates MYB61 from the
~125 other Arabidopsis R2R3-MYBs), **and** an independent MYB call from §13.

| | sugarcane (R570) | purple (LA purple) |
|---|---|---|
| candidates (qcov ≥ 50%) | 21 | 29 |
| pass RBH **and** MYB rule | 11 | 16 |
| recovered by the tree | +1 | 0 |
| **MYB61 copies** | **12** | **16** |
| co-orthologue clades | chr02 (chr3-type) + chr06 (chr9-type) | chr03 + chr09 |

The copies sit in **2 co-orthologue clades** joined by **5 shared orthogroups**,
and all 27 strictly-confirmed copies fall inside a single **32-tip grass MYB61
clade** (MAFFT + FastTree, with rejected-MYB and Arabidopsis-paralogue decoys) —
so "MYB61 in sugarcane" and "MYB61 in purple" are the same evolutionary entity
and the two networks are comparable at this locus.

Note the purple copies are on **chromosome 9**, matching both the sorghum chr9
co-orthologue and the chromosome in Kiet's id — but at loci `09G00032xx–09G00038xx`,
not `09G0002230`.

### 14c. What MYB61 actually does in each network

> **Reading "on conserved edges".** This is a **binary gene-level flag**: the gene
> has **at least one** conserved edge (`network_conservation_join.r` unions both
> endpoints of every conserved edge). It is *not* the fraction of that gene's
> edges that are conserved. That is why ~25–37% of *genes* are flagged while only
> 1.5–10.5% of *edges* are conserved (§7) — different denominators: 10.3M
> conserved purple edges flag up to ~20.7M endpoints across only 170k nodes, so a
> small share of edges saturates a large share of nodes.

| | sugarcane | purple |
|---|---|---|
| copies / in network | 12 / 8 | 16 / 15 |
| median degree percentile | **45.7** | **45.1** |
| copies N-correlated (\|r\|≥0.6, padj≤0.05) | **0** | **0** |
| strongest \|r\| with nitrogen | 0.247 | 0.643 (padj 0.27, n.s.) |
| on conserved edges | 37.5% vs 37.2% bg — p=0.62 | **60% vs 24.9% bg, 2.4×, p=0.0041** |
| …vs **degree-matched** null | 3 obs vs 2.92 exp — p=0.62 | **9 obs vs 3.41 exp, 2.6×, p=0.0019** |

- **MYB61 is not a hub.** Every copy sits near the median of its own network's
  degree distribution (percentile ~45 in both), not in the tail.
- **MYB61 is not significantly nitrogen-coupled in either dataset.** The single
  copy exceeding |r|≥0.6 (`Soffic.03G0011310-3B`, r = −0.643) has padj = 0.27.
  This is a direct, quantitative tension with Kiet et al.'s central claim,
  measured in the same reference they used.
- **But in purple it is enriched on cross-species conserved edges** (2.4×,
  exact binomial p = 0.0041) — while in sugarcane it tracks the background
  exactly. So MYB61's signal here is *conserved wiring*, not *N response*, and
  the enrichment is asymmetric between the two studies.
- The enrichment is **not a degree artifact**. "On a conserved edge" is a binary
  ≥1-edge flag and is strongly degree-dependent (purple: 3.2% of nodes in the
  lowest degree decile vs 43.3% in the highest), so the all-node null is not
  sufficient alone. Against a **degree-matched null** (20,000 resamples of nodes
  of near-identical degree) purple MYB61 gives 9 observed vs 3.41 expected,
  **2.6×, empirical p = 0.0019** — slightly *stronger* than the naive test,
  because the copies sit at moderate degree (median 522, ~decile 5) where the
  background rate is only ~22%. Sugarcane stays flat (3 vs 2.92, p = 0.62).

Caveats stated explicitly: OrthoFinder leaves many polyploid haplotype copies
**unassigned** (6/12 sugarcane, 3/16 purple are singletons), so absence from
`Orthogroups.tsv` is not evidence of absence — per-copy status is in
`MYB61_bridge_status.tsv`. And `SoffiXsponR570.02Ag129700` is a genuine MYB61
copy whose gene model retains only one Myb repeat, so the strict rules called it
`MYB-related`; the tree recovers it and it is counted separately. Only 12 of the
23 in-network copies have a `treatment` correlation at all (that statistic is
defined only on the conserved-gene sets).

(A: degree ECDF with every copy marked · B: N correlation per copy · C:
conserved-edge % vs three backgrounds).

### 14d. Testing Kiet et al.'s claim on their own leaf samples (`08_myb61_expression_test.r`)

§14c used a **Pearson correlation against treatment coded 0/2/6, pooled over both
genotypes** (`gene_trait_cor.r:55`). Kiet et al. describe ScMYB61.1 as
**non-monotonic** (highest at *both* low and high N, lowest at normal) and
**opposite between genotypes** — a shape a linear correlation has ~zero power
against, and a genotype split that pooling cancels. So §14c's null result was
partly a blind spot of the test. Re-tested with the design their claim predicts,
on the same 18 leaf libraries (2 genotypes × 0N/2N/6N × 3 reps): per-genotype
one-way ANOVA, a **U-shape contrast** `c(+1,−2,+1)`, a linear contrast, and a
genotype × N interaction.

**A real, significant non-monotonic N response exists — in the opposite
direction to the published description.**

| 51NG3 (the genotype Kiet highlight) | value |
|---|---|
| copies testable / 16 | 13 |
| ANOVA p<0.05 (BH padj<0.05) | 9 (7) |
| U-shape contrast padj<0.05 | **10** |
| sign of the U estimate | **11 of 13 negative** |
| linear contrast p<0.05 | **0** |
| TAGZ: anything significant | **0 of 13** |

A **negative** U estimate means expression peaks at **normal** N and drops at
both extremes — an *inverted* U, whereas Kiet describe the *upright* U (peaks at
0N and 6N). The interaction term confirms the genotypes genuinely differ
(`09F0003800-9F` padj = 7.2e-05; four more at padj < 0.01), so their
"opposite patterns between genotypes" claim is supported in shape even though
the direction in 51NG3 is not. And crucially the **linear contrast is
significant for 0 copies** — direct confirmation that §14c's Pearson test was
structurally unable to see this, not that nothing was there.

**The decisive caveat: these copies are barely expressed in leaf.** Mean TPM in
51NG3 is 0.0–1.5, with only **2 of 16** copies above 1 TPM. At that level TPM
ratios are unstable, so the significant U-contrasts rest on very small absolute
differences. This is suggestive, not conclusive — and it is leaf-only, whereas
Kiet emphasise roots as well.

**The published locus is expressed far more than the real gene.** As a negative
control, `Soffic.09G0002230-9G` — the DUF1218 gene at Kiet's published id, and a
network hub (degree 18,775) — runs at **79.6 / 44.0 mean TPM**, one to two orders
of magnitude above any genuine MYB61 copy. Combined with §14e below, the most
parsimonious reading is that the study's transcriptomic signal and its cloned
gene are **two different loci**.

### 14e. The cloning primers identify the gene, and they match ours

The paper's forward primer is `GGATCC` (BamHI) + `ATGGGGAGGCATTCTTGC`, i.e. a
protein starting **M-G-R-H-S-C**. **All 28** MYB61 copies identified here
(12 sugarcane + 16 purple) begin `MGRHSCCYKQKL`. **None** of the copies at the
published locus do — `09G0002230-9A/-9B/-9C/-9D/-9G` start `MARPPTMVIQDD`,
`MDRSWIFGIKFT`, `MVHPETMLLHEP`, `MARRRRLSASSL`, `MALSASLRISLI`, and
`03G0002230-3D` starts `MLLLIAHAAAAC`.

So the gene Kiet et al. actually **cloned and overexpressed is one of the copies
found here** — independent, sequence-level confirmation that the homology search
identified the right gene, and that the published id is simply wrong. Their
Arabidopsis overexpression phenotype therefore stands on the real MYB61; what
does not follow is the link between that phenotype and the RNA-seq expression
attributed to `Soff.09G0002230–3D`.

## 15. Muñoz Module 20 across both networks (`scripts/module20/`) — H1 step 3

Muñoz-Perez et al. build their nitrogen claim on **Module 20**: 12 genes, ~75%
MYB/MYB-related TAPs, high betweenness in *their* de novo network. Do those
genes keep the same characteristics in **our** two reference networks?

Module 20 was *defined* as MYB-rich and central, so "is it MYB-rich and
central?" is circular. The testable questions are whether the members map,
whether **our own** TF calls agree, whether they are central **against the MYB
background**, whether they are N-responsive in **our** re-quantification, and
whether any of it survives in purple.

### 15a. Their classification is exactly right — verified at the source

Muñoz's proteome (`raw_sugarcane/transcriptome_munoz/sugar_cane.pep`) is
**byte-identical to `GET_TFS/db/sugar_cane.pep`**, the proteome the original
GET_TFS run used — so their `Family` column comes from the same PlnTFDB rule
method we use. Running our pipeline (`hmmsearch --cut_ga` +
`assign_family_membership.pl` + `RulesFull`) on **their** proteins reproduces
**12 / 12** of their calls, including **9 / 9** MYB-family members and all 3
`no_tf`. **"75% MYB" is correct**, and every disagreement downstream is about
the orthologs we map to, not about how TFs are classified.

### 15b. Mapping, and a serious independence problem

Mapping is `blastp` (Muñoz proteins → each reference proteome) plus the
reciprocal-Arabidopsis filter of §14b. **10 of 12 members map in sugarcane**
(33 genes, 30 in network), **6 in purple** (27 genes, 25 in network).

The 12 members are **not 12 independent genes**:

- **2 have no predicted ORF at all** (`N_R___TRINITY_DN20_c0_g1_i6`,
  `N_R___TRINITY_DN331_c0_g1_i3`) — non-coding / UTR fragments, which is why
  they never mapped; that is a result, not a failure.
- `SCA3__SP803280_c111248_g1_i2` / `_i4` are **isoforms of one locus**, and they
  anchor to *different* Arabidopsis genes — that locus is likely chimeric.
- The 9 MYB members resolve to just **two MYB orthologue groups**:
  **AT5G59780 (AtMYB59)** ×5 and **AT3G46130 (AtMYB48)** ×3.

They collapse to **3–4 independent units**. Deduplicating haplotype copies moved
the sugarcane centrality result from **p = 2.8e-05 to p = 0.118**, so all tests
are reported at gene / locus / **anchor** level and the anchor level is the one
to read.

*(An earlier revision searched the nucleotide contigs with `blastx`. The
protein bridge is a strict subset — sugarcane 33 of 34 genes, purple 27 of 36 —
i.e. more conservative, as expected once spurious frame hits are removed. The
`blastx` maps are kept in `work/*_blastx.tsv`.)*

### 15c. What maps is mostly not the MYB part

| in-network genes by Arabidopsis anchor | sugarcane | purple |
|---|---|---|
| `AT3G55960` (uncharacterised) | 11 | 12 |
| `AT4G10770` (**OPT7**, oligopeptide transporter, `no_tf`) | 10 | 11 |
| `AT5G59780` (**MYB59**) | 9 | **2** |
| `AT3G46130` (**MYB48**) | **0** | **0** |

The members that map in volume are the two **non-MYB** anchors; the MYB48 group
reaches neither network. So only **30%** (sugarcane) and **8%** (purple) of the
mapped in-network genes carry a MYB call — despite their own classification
reproducing perfectly. **The MYB layer of Module 20 is thin in R570 and
essentially absent in LA purple (2 genes).**

# Hypotheses

Both source papers independently conclude that **MYB transcription factors are
the central regulators of the nitrogen response** (Muñoz: Module 20, 75% MYB
TAPs; Ta Quang Kiet: the whole paper converges on ScMYB61.1). Yet the
comparative pipeline finds co-expression **topology is essentially not
conserved** (edge Jaccard 0.015–0.105; 1 ortholog pair N-responsive on both
sides; 0 treatment-conserved edges). That gap frames the questions below.

**H0 — Is the cross-species conservation call real, or a mapping artifact?**
*Tested (§12 above).* Conserved-edge genes are enriched, against each network's
own nodes, for a coherent conserved core — translation, proteasome, splicing,
photosynthesis/C4 (PEPCase, GOGAT). **Result: real biology, not noise.** The
near-zero edge conservation is a genuine finding. Caveat: this test is
conditioned on topological conservation, so it cannot by itself rank
"function vs topology" conservation — it only shows the conserved backbone is
primary-metabolism/core machinery.

**H1 — Is the conserved core the MYB regulatory layer, not the topology?**
*In progress — steps 1–2 done (§13, §14).* TFs are catalogued in both networks
and MYB + MYB-related is the #2–3 family block in each (sugarcane 843, purple
1,305 in-network genes), so a substantial, comparable MYB layer exists. MYB61
itself has now been located from sequence in both references (12 + 16 copies,
5 shared orthogroups, one grass MYB61 clade) after showing Kiet's published id
is unresolvable.

**The answer so far is: partly, and not the way either paper frames it.** MYB61
is neither a hub (median degree percentile ~45 in both) nor significantly
nitrogen-correlated in *either* dataset (0 copies at |r|≥0.6 & padj≤0.05,
best padj = 0.27) — measured in the same reference Kiet et al. used. What it
*is*, in purple only, is significantly enriched on cross-species conserved edges
(2.4×, p=0.0041), with sugarcane at background. So the MYB signal looks like
conserved wiring rather than N response, and it is asymmetric between studies.

Re-tested against the design Kiet's claim actually predicts (§14d), a
**significant non-monotonic N response does exist** in 51NG3 (U-contrast padj<0.05
for 10/13 testable copies) — but **inverted** relative to their description
(peak at normal N, not at the extremes), with **0** copies showing a linear
response, confirming the earlier Pearson test was structurally blind to it. The
copies are barely expressed in leaf (only 2/16 above 1 TPM), so this is
suggestive rather than conclusive. Their cloning primers (§14e) prove the gene
they cloned is one of ours, so their Arabidopsis overexpression phenotype rests
on the real MYB61 — while the RNA-seq signal attributed to `Soff.09G0002230–3D`
belongs to a different, far more highly expressed non-MYB locus.

Muñoz's Module 20 has now had the same treatment (§15), mapped protein-level
from their own proteome. Their classification is **exactly right** — our method
reproduces 12/12 of their calls on their own sequences — and the genes are
**robustly nitrogen-responsive in their data** (24/33 significant, validating
their claim). But the module does **not** transfer as structure: its MYB
component barely reaches our references (MYB59 → 9 sugarcane / **2** purple
genes; MYB48 → none), centrality is indistinguishable from ordinary MYBs in both
networks (anchor-level p = 0.44 / 0.72), there is no conserved-edge enrichment,
nothing survives BH correction in purple, and the module is only partially
recovered in sugarcane (`Module_016`). The 12 published members also collapse to
**3–4 independent units** (two MYB orthologue groups; 2 members are non-coding).

**Converging answer to H1: the conserved core is the nitrogen *response*, not the
MYB regulatory *topology*.** Both papers' central MYB claims survive as
expression responses and fail as network structure — matching H0's finding that
the conserved backbone is primary metabolism, not regulatory wiring.

**H2 — Does conservation live at the function/pathway level, not the edge
level?** *Partially addressed by H0.* The apples-to-apples version: enrich each
species' **full** network (genome background) and compare the functional-term
Jaccard against the edge Jaccard on the same unconditioned universe. If
functional overlap ≫ edge overlap, function is the more conserved layer.

**H3 — Do hub genes conserve even when edges do not?** *Not yet tested.*
Rank-correlate degree/betweenness across ortholog pairs (Muñoz stresses hub
TAPs). Cheap — uses existing node-metrics tables.

**H4 — Is the "responsive/non-responsive" axis mis-aligned between studies?**
*Not yet tested.* In Muñoz "responsive" = coordinated R genotype; in Ta Quang
Kiet the better N-stress performer (51NG3, wild) is the *broad-response* one
while the commercial genotype is low-plasticity. Transcriptional plasticity may
map R↔51NG3, not R↔commercial — so pooling genotypes could wash out
conservation. Test by stratifying the conservation analysis by genotype pairing.
